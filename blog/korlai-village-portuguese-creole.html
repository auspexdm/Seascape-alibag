Korlai village and its Portuguese creole

The village on our doorstep still speaks a language born in the 1500s.

Journal · Local culture · 5 min read

Korlai is a fishing village at the mouth of the Kundalika river, between Alibag and Murud, where around 1,000 people still speak Kristi, a Portuguese-based creole that grew out of the 1500s. It is a few minutes from The Seascape, and it is one of the most unusual places on the Konkan coast. Here is what to know before you walk in.

Where Korlai is

Korlai sits on the estuary where the Kundalika meets the Arabian Sea, roughly 150 km south of Mumbai in Raigad district. Revdanda is across the water to the north. The village spreads along the foot of the hill that carries Korlai Fort, with fishing boats moored along the creek.

Kristi: the language of Korlai

Ask a villager what they speak and you may hear Kristi, or nou ling, "our language". Linguists call it Korlai Indo-Portuguese Creole. It took shape in the 1500s, when the Portuguese held this coast and Catholic converts, traders and soldiers settled around the fort. Its vocabulary is largely Portuguese, while its sounds and grammar lean on Marathi.

The Portuguese held Chaul and Korlai until 1740, when the Marathas took the area, yet the language stayed. Today it is spoken mainly at home, by a community of roughly 1,000 people. Younger speakers increasingly use Marathi, which is why researchers treat Kristi as a language to document and protect.

The fort and its church

The Portuguese built Korlai Fort, also called Morro or Castle Curlew, on the hill above the village to guard the river mouth. Inside the walls are the ruins of a church, Nossa Senhora do Mar, "Our Lady of the Sea", recorded on the hill by 1630, and an automated lighthouse built in 1955 stands at the southern end. Visiting respectfully

  • It is a home, not a museum. Korlai is a working village. Keep voices down in the lanes and ask before photographing people or houses.
  • Go early. Walk the village and climb to the fort in the cooler morning, then come back for breakfast.
  • Dress for a climb. Shoes with grip, water and sun cover for the open hillside.
  • Look at the boats. The creek is busiest when the fishing boats come in; fresh catch from this coast ends up on Konkani tables nearby, including at our in-house kitchen.

Frequently asked questions

What language is spoken in Korlai?

Alongside Marathi, many families speak Korlai Indo-Portuguese Creole, locally called Kristi or nou ling ("our language"), a Portuguese-based creole that developed in the 1500s.

How many people speak Korlai Creole?

Roughly 1,000 people in Korlai village, mostly at home. Younger speakers increasingly use Marathi.

Where is Korlai?

At the mouth of the Kundalika river in Raigad district, Maharashtra, between Alibag and Murud and about 150 km south of Mumbai.
